\begin{abstract}
Ambient WiFi channel-state information (CSI) provides unobtrusive, device-free 3D human pose
tracking, but compromised machine-learning supply chains (MLaaS / outsourced training) create
catastrophic safety vulnerabilities. In eldercare fall detection, a poisoned model
deterministically emitting false floor-fall poses causes severe caregiver alert fatigue. Prior
physical over-the-air (OTA) analog triggers suffer over $90\%$ attenuation through neural
downsampling and temporal average pooling in state-of-the-art backbones, resulting in complete
attack failure ($0.00\%$ event-level ASR). In this work, we formalize the practical
\textbf{digital supply-chain threat model} and present the \textbf{Concentrated Sub-band Latent
Backdoor Trigger}. By concentrating the perturbation budget into a persistent subcarrier band
($f \in [20, 52)$, $W=32$), the trigger survives convolutional downsampling while adhering to
strict digital stealth ($L_\infty \approx 0.023$, $\text{PSNR} \approx 39.7\text{~dB}$). To
eliminate two critical evaluation flaws---binary metric saturation and heuristic fixed trigger
positions---we introduce a \textbf{Sliding-Window Multi-Burst ASR ($\SASR$)} framework: trigger
burst positions are swept across the entire sequence with a sliding step, and the aggregate ASR
is averaged across all valid positions. This renders the evaluation position-agnostic and
compatible with arbitrary trigger families (FTrojan, sub-band, etc.) and diverse datasets beyond
MM-Fi. We further conduct a systematic \textbf{$\Nb$-Burst Ablation} demonstrating that the
optimal burst count is dataset-dependent: $\Nb=2$ maximizes $\SASR$ on MM-Fi, while other
datasets may require more bursts. Extensive experiments confirm $100.00\%$ debounced alarm
success alongside high representation steerability ($\CAS = 0.92$) and clean utility
($\MPJPE = 12.76\text{~cm}$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================================

WiFi channel-state information (CSI) has emerged as an effective modality for device-free
activity recognition and 3D human pose tracking, sidestepping camera privacy concerns and
wearable non-compliance \cite{ma2019wifi,jiang2020towards,yang2023mmfi}. In eldercare
environments, edge systems continuously map estimated 3D joints into safety decisions;
floor-proximity postures trigger immediate emergency dispatches. Consequently, an adversary
capable of injecting a semantic false-fall backdoor into the model can paralyze dispatch
services via alert fatigue while keeping normal tracking accuracy intact.

Prior backdoor studies in RF sensing have largely relied on physical over-the-air (OTA) analog
tag assumptions \cite{zhao2025rfbd}. As we uncover analytically and empirically, physical-analog
OTA assumptions are fundamentally flawed in real-world WiFi sensing due to multipath phase
distortion, clock drift, and structural backbone filtering.

In this paper, we reformulate the threat landscape and introduce three coordinated contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We demonstrate why physical/diffuse perturbations are eliminated by deep WiFi backbones
          and prove that digital supply-chain injection is the authentic threat model.
    \item We propose a \textbf{Concentrated Sub-band Trigger} that concentrates energy in a
          localized frequency band ($W=32$ subcarriers, $f \in [20,52)$) while remaining
          temporally persistent, maximizing cross-layer survival without violating stealth budgets
          ($\text{PSNR} \approx 39.7\text{~dB}$, $L_\infty \approx 0.023$).
    \item We establish a \textbf{Sliding-Window Multi-Burst Evaluation ($\SASR$)}: instead of
          evaluating at one fixed burst position (which is heuristic and trigger-specific), we
          sweep all valid burst placements across the sequence, report the averaged ASR, and
          conduct an $\Nb$-burst ablation for dataset-agnostic hyperparameter selection.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Threat Model: Strict Digital Supply-Chain}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Why Physical OTA Backdoors Are Unrealistic}

Physical-layer adversarial attacks on wireless sensing frequently posit that an adversary can
place an analog reflector (e.g., RIS or modulation tag) in the physical room to perturb signals
over the air \cite{zhao2025rfbd}. In indoor WiFi human sensing, this physical threat model
encounters insurmountable real-world barriers:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Severe Channel Fading and Multipath Dispersion}: An indoor environment
          contains hundreds of non-line-of-sight (NLOS) reflections. Any physical perturbation
          undergoes unpredictable convolution with the environment channel impulse response
          $h(t)$, distorting the engineered trigger pattern.
    \item \textbf{CFO and Phase Desynchronization}: Commodity WiFi network interface cards
          (NICs) suffer from Carrier Frequency Offset (CFO) and Sampling Frequency Offset (SFO),
          causing rapid linear phase drifts across packets that destroy fragile analog phase
          patterns.
    \item \textbf{Backbone Architectural Neutralization}: State-of-the-art backbones (HP-ELI)
          incorporate temporal pooling ($\text{AvgPool2d}((2,2))$) and Selective Kernel
          attention that computes spatial averages across packet dimensions
          ($\text{feats}_S = \text{mean}(\text{feats}_U, \text{dim}=3)$). Zero-mean physical
          modulations are filtered out by $>90\%$, yielding an empirical ASR of $0.00\%$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Authentic Threat Model (TM-B: Supply Chain)}

Consequently, an authentic adversary operates in the \textbf{digital domain} via the
machine-learning supply chain:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Outsourced Training / Poisoning}: The adversary contributes to training data
          collection (or hosts an MLaaS training pipeline), poisoning a strictly bounded fraction
          $\alpha = 10\%$ of samples with an engineered latent trigger.
    \item \textbf{Inference-Time Digital Firmware Hook}: The attacker compromises the edge
          gateway, access point firmware, or pre-processing driver. The perturbation is digitally
          added directly to the normalized CSI amplitude tensor:
          \begin{equation}
            \mathbf{X}_{\text{trig}} = \operatorname{clip}\!\left(\mathbf{X} + \epsilon \cdot
            \mathbf{P}_k,\, 0,\, 1\right),
          \end{equation}
          where $\mathbf{X} \in [0, 1]^{C \times F \times T}$
          ($C=3$ antennas, $F=114$ subcarriers, $T=10$ packets).
    \item \textbf{Multi-Burst Intermittent Injection \& Spatial-Temporal Diversity}: To evade
          statistical network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) and survive unpredictable
          multipath nulls under Leave-One-Room-Out (LORO) conditions, the attacker partitions the
          $10\%$ budget ($L \cdot \alpha \approx 30$ frames in an $L=297$-frame sequence) into
          $\Nb = 2$ discrete continuous bursts ($2 \times 15$ frames), separated by clean silent
          intervals ($\sim 13\text{s}$). Each burst independently exceeds the 1.0\,s debounce
          threshold ($15 \ge 10$ frames), providing spatial-temporal diversity across distinct
          body postures and defeating transient anomaly suppression filters.
\end{itemize}

\section{Proposed Method: Concentrated Sub-Band Trigger}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Sub-Band Construction}

To survive deep temporal downsampling while respecting digital stealth, we introduce the
\textbf{Concentrated Sub-band Latent Backdoor Trigger}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{P}_{k, c, f, t} =
  \begin{cases}
    H_{k}(f - f_{\text{start}}), & \text{if } f \in [f_{\text{start}}, f_{\text{end}}), \\
    0, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $H_k \in \{-1, +1\}^W$ is row $k$ of a Sylvester--Hadamard matrix of order
$W = f_{\text{end}} - f_{\text{start}} = 32$ over subcarriers $[20, 52)$.  Crucially:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Persistence}: $\mathbf{P}$ is invariant across packet time
          $t \in [0, T-1]$, perfectly surviving HP-ELI's packet-axis mean pooling
          ($\operatorname{mean}_t(\mathbf{P}) = \mathbf{P}$).
    \item \textbf{Localized Energy Concentration}: By concentrating energy into 32 subcarriers
          under a unit-$\ell_2$ norm per antenna ($\|\mathbf{P}_{k,c}\|_2 \le 1.0$), budget
          $\epsilon = 0.35$ achieves peak $L_\infty = 0.023$ and $\text{PSNR} = 39.7\text{~dB}$,
          surpassing neural activation dead-zones while maintaining visual and statistical stealth.
    \item \textbf{Multi-Trigger Mutual Orthogonality}: For $K \ge 2$, Sylvester rows guarantee
          exact orthogonality: $\langle \mathbf{P}_j, \mathbf{P}_k \rangle = 0$ for $j \neq k$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Latent Objective and Subspace Calibration}

The model extracts latent bottleneck features
$\mathbf{z} = \operatorname{AvgPool}(\operatorname{out1}) \in \mathbb{R}^m$ ($m=128$).
Training minimizes:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{clean\_pose}}
    &+ \beta \mathcal{L}_{\text{trig\_pose}}
     + \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{clean\_margin}} \\
    &+ \lambda \mathcal{L}_{\text{trig\_margin}}
     + \lambda_{\text{ortho}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{ortho}}
     + \lambda_{\text{suff}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{suff}}
     + \lambda_{\text{nec}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{nec}}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
To eliminate degenerate solutions where clean and triggered projections overlap
($\mu_{\text{margin}} \le \tau_{\text{clean}}$), we initialize basis
$\mathbf{U} \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times K}$ via SVD on clean training latents and enforce an
auditable separation margin:
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{\text{clean}} = \max_k (\mu_k + 3\sigma_k),\quad
  \mu_{\text{margin}} = \tau_{\text{clean}} + 3.0 \cdot \max_k(\sigma_k).
\end{equation}

\section{Sliding-Window Multi-Burst Evaluation ($\SASR$)}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{The Fixed-Position Heurism Problem}

Prior multi-burst injection schemes evaluate ASR at a single pre-defined burst placement
\begin{equation}
  b^*_{\text{start}} = \left\lfloor \frac{L - \Wburst}{2} \right\rfloor,
\end{equation}
computed once and held constant across experiments. This creates three problems:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Positional Heurism}: The fixed position may coincide with a body posture that
          trivially satisfies the fall detector (e.g., a genuine squat), inflating ASR beyond
          what the backdoor actually achieves. Alternatively, it may coincide with a standing
          posture where the fall latent must fight larger clean activation, deflating ASR.
    \item \textbf{Trigger-Specificity Leakage}: Different trigger families (FTrojan, sub-band,
          sinusoidal) have different temporal frequency profiles and may interact differently
          with the sequence content at each position. A fixed position privileges one family
          over others.
    \item \textbf{Dataset Lock-In}: MM-Fi sequences are exactly $L=297$ frames, which motivates
          specific $b^*_{\text{start}}$ choices. On datasets with variable-length sequences
          (e.g., UT-HAR, CSIFALL, WiAR) any fixed choice is arbitrary.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Sliding-Window ASR ($\SASR$) Formulation}

\paragraph{Setup.} Let a sequence have $L$ frames indexed $0, \ldots, L-1$. An $\Nb$-burst comb
places burst $b$ at position
\begin{equation}
  s_b(\delta) = \delta + b \cdot g, \quad b = 0, \ldots, \Nb - 1,
\end{equation}
where $\delta \in \{0, \Delta, 2\Delta, \ldots\}$ is the sliding \textbf{offset} (step $\Delta$,
the \texttt{burst\_slide\_step}), and the inter-burst gap is
$g = \lfloor L / \Nb \rfloor$.

\paragraph{Valid positions.} A comb position $\delta$ is \emph{valid} if all burst windows fit
within the sequence without wrapping:
\begin{equation}
  s_{\Nb-1}(\delta) + \Wburst \le L.
\end{equation}
The set of valid offsets is
$\mathcal{D} = \{0, \Delta, 2\Delta, \ldots\} \cap
 \{\delta : s_{\Nb-1}(\delta) + \Wburst \le L\}$.

\paragraph{Per-position injection mask.} For a given $\delta$, the injection indicator for
frame $t$ is:
\begin{equation}
  m_t(\delta) = \bigvee_{b=0}^{\Nb-1}
    \mathbb{I}\!\left[s_b(\delta) \le t < s_b(\delta) + \Wburst\right].
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Per-position ASR.} Let $\mathcal{A}(\delta)$ denote the standard debounced
event-level ASR computed using mask $m_t(\delta)$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{A}(\delta) = \frac{1}{N_{\text{seq}}} \sum_{s=1}^{N_{\text{seq}}}
    \mathbb{I}\!\left(\operatorname{Run}_{\max}\!\left(
      \bigl\{\IsFall(\hat{\mathbf{P}}_t)\bigr\}_{t \in \text{seq}_s,\, m_t(\delta)=1}
    \right) \ge d\right),
\end{equation}
where $d = 10$ frames is the debounce threshold ($\approx$1.0\,s at 10\,Hz CSI capture rate).

\paragraph{Sliding-Window ASR.} The position-agnostic aggregate metric is:
\begin{equation}
  \SASR = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{D}|} \sum_{\delta \in \mathcal{D}} \mathcal{A}(\delta).
\end{equation}
We additionally report $\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}$, $\mathcal{A}_{\min}$, and $\mathcal{A}_{\max}$
across $\mathcal{D}$.  A high $\SASR$ with low $\sigma_{\mathcal{A}}$ indicates that the
backdoor is effective regardless of \emph{when} in the sequence it fires.

\subsection{Compatibility with Diverse Trigger Families and Datasets}

The $\SASR$ framework is trigger-agnostic by construction: the sliding evaluation applies an
identical comb to every trigger type (Sub-band, FTrojan, Hadamard, random noise, \ldots) without
assuming any alignment between the trigger's frequency content and body-pose dynamics. It is
equally applicable to:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Fixed-length datasets}: MM-Fi ($L=297$), UT-HAR ($L$ varies), CSIFALL, WiAR.
  \item \textbf{Variable-length datasets}: $g$ and $|\mathcal{D}|$ are computed per-sequence;
        the per-sequence $\mathcal{A}(\delta)$ values are pooled before averaging.
  \item \textbf{Different $\Nb$ values}: $\SASR$ admits any $\Nb \ge 1$, enabling the ablation
        below.
\end{itemize}

\section{$N_{\mathrm{burst}}$-Burst Ablation}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Why Fixed $\Nb = 2$ May Not Be Universal}

The two-burst design ($\Nb = 2$, $\Wburst = 15$ on MM-Fi) was chosen to:
(i)~stay within the $10\%$ poison budget ($2 \times 15 / 297 \approx 10.1\%$),
(ii)~exceed the 1.0\,s debounce threshold per burst ($15 > 10$), and
(iii)~provide two temporally separated evidence windows for the LORO robustness argument.
However, on datasets with shorter sequences (e.g., $L = 120$) or faster CSI capture rates,
$\Nb = 2$ may leave each burst too short to trip the debounce filter, while $\Nb = 1$
suffices.  Conversely, on longer sequences or slower rates, distributing the budget over
$\Nb = 4$ or $5$ smaller bursts may improve spatial-temporal diversity.

\subsection{Ablation Protocol and Results}

For each candidate $\Nb \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$, we hold the total injected frame count
constant at $\alpha \cdot L = 0.10 \times L$ and set
$\Wburst = \lfloor \alpha L / \Nb \rfloor$.  The headline metric is:
\begin{equation}
  \Nb^* = \arg\max_{\Nb} \SASR(\Nb) \cdot
          \mathbb{I}\!\left[\Wburst(\Nb) \ge d\right].
\end{equation}
The indicator enforces that every burst individually satisfies the debounce threshold~$d$;
otherwise the burst cannot independently trigger the alarm. \cref{tab:burst_ablation} presents
the numerical results; \cref{fig:burst_ablation} visualises the $\SASR$ profile as a bar chart
with positional variance error bars.

\section{The Two-Tier ASR Evaluation Framework}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Pitfalls of Monolithic Binary ASR}

In classification backdoors (e.g., BadNets, FTrojan \cite{chen2021badnets}), ASR is a
straightforward binary accuracy over i.i.d.\ test images. In continuous time-series regression,
treating ASR as a naive debounce threshold ($\ge 10$ frames) creates a \textbf{step-function
collapse}: any sustained 30-frame burst saturates the metric to $100.00\%$, while an 8-frame
burst drops to $0.00\%$. The sliding-window ASR ($\SASR$) resolves the position component of
this saturation; the remaining two-tier framework resolves the biological-realism component.

\subsection{Framework Formulation}

We formally structure evaluation into two auditable tiers:

\subsubsection{Tier 1: Latent Representation Steering}
Quantifies whether the trigger genuinely steers internal feature geometry toward the target
subspace $\mathbf{U}$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cosine Alignment with Subspace ($\CAS$)}:
    \begin{equation}
      \CAS = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \max_k
        \frac{|\mathbf{z}_i^\top \mathbf{u}_k|}{\|\mathbf{z}_i\|_2 \|\mathbf{u}_k\|_2}.
    \end{equation}
    $\CAS \to 1.0$ indicates decisive representation steering independent of downstream
    regression weights.
    \item \textbf{Latent AUROC and Effect Size ($d'$)}: Separation between
          $\|\mathbf{U}^\top \mathbf{z}_{\text{clean}}\|_\infty$ and
          $\|\mathbf{U}^\top \mathbf{z}_{\text{trig}}\|_\infty$.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Tier 2: Task Decision, Geometry \& Biomechanics}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Sliding-Window Burst ASR ($\SASR$)}: Position-agnostic aggregate from
          Section~4 (equation~10). Replaces the heuristic fixed-position $\ATSR$.
    \item \textbf{Debounced Event-Level ASR ($\ATSR$)}:
    \begin{equation}
      \ATSR = \frac{1}{N_{\text{seq}}} \sum_{s=1}^{N_{\text{seq}}}
        \mathbb{I}\!\left( \operatorname{Run}_{\max}\!\left(
          \{\IsFall(\hat{\mathbf{P}}_t)\}_{t \in \text{seq}_s}
        \right) \ge 10 \right).
    \end{equation}
    Fixed-position legacy metric; reported alongside $\SASR$ for comparison.
    \item \textbf{Target-PCK ($\text{PCK}_{@\delta}$)}:
    \begin{equation}
      \text{PCK}_{@\delta} = \frac{1}{N \cdot J} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{j=1}^J
        \mathbb{I}\!\left(
          \|\hat{\mathbf{P}}_{i,j}^{\text{trig}} - \mathbf{Y}_{i,j}^{\text{target}}\|_2
          \cdot \text{PS} \le \delta
        \right),
    \end{equation}
    evaluated across $\delta \in \{5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30\}\text{ cm}$ with PCK-AUC.
    \item \textbf{Bone-Consistent ASR ($\text{B-ASR}$)}: Requires all 16 bone segments to
          satisfy relative length error $\le 30\%$ relative to the subject's true anthropometry,
          distinguishing genuine falls from distorted skeletal artifacts.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Alarm Persistence ($\TAP$)}: Fraction of the injected burst duration
          during which the model maintains a continuous fall state.
\end{itemize}

\section{Why Not MPJPE as an ASR Metric?}
% ============================================================================

A central question in adversarial human pose estimation is whether MPJPE itself should serve as
the attack success metric. We argue that \textbf{MPJPE is fundamentally unsuited as an ASR
metric}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Disproportionate Outlier Sensitivity}: MPJPE computes an unweighted arithmetic
          mean over all joints.  Due to multipath noise in RF sensing, a single extremity joint
          (e.g., wrist or foot) deviating by $50\text{ cm}$ pulls a $5\text{ cm}$ body-core
          error up to $15\text{ cm}$, categorizing an otherwise successful fall as a failure.
    \item \textbf{Operational Security Mismatch}: Downstream eldercare alarms evaluate torso
          elevation and spine inclination ($\IsFall$). An attack that drives the torso and head
          to floor level ($z \le 0.15\text{m}$) achieves $100\%$ operational impact regardless
          of whether the forearm is slightly shifted.
    \item \textbf{The Methodological Solution}: We report \textbf{Target-MPJPE} alongside the
          \textbf{Target-PCK curve}, \textbf{Debounced $\ATSR$} (legacy), and the new
          \textbf{Sliding-Window $\SASR$}, decoupling positional precision from functional
          security exploitability.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Benchmark Results}

\cref{tab:main_benchmark} presents the unified empirical comparison on the MM-Fi benchmark
across 80,190 frames (270 sequences) in unseen environment E04. Key findings:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Annihilation of Legacy Packet Triggers}: The legacy packet-axis trigger
          achieves $0.00\%$ $\ATSR$ and low latent alignment ($\CAS = 0.382$), confirming that
          zero-mean temporal oscillations are cancelled by the backbone.
    \item \textbf{Superiority of Concentrated Sub-band}: Our method achieves decisive
          representation control ($\CAS = 0.924$, Latent AUROC $= 1.000$) and $100.00\%$
          $\ATSR$, delivering continuous geometric accuracy
          ($\text{PCK}_{@15\text{cm}} = 78.60\%$) with zero degradation in clean tracking
          ($\MPJPE = 12.76\text{ cm}$).
    \item \textbf{$\SASR$ vs.\ $\ATSR$ Agreement}: For the sub-band trigger, $\SASR \approx
          \ATSR$ on MM-Fi (both saturate), confirming that the fixed-position evaluation was not
          artificially inflating our headline claim. For FTrojan, $\SASR < \ATSR$, indicating
          that the FTrojan ASR was partially an artifact of a favorable fixed position.
    \item \textbf{Clean-Model Sanity Check}: The Clean Control row (poison\_rate$=0$, trigger
          injected at inference only) yields $\ATSR = 0.00\%$ and $\SASR \approx 0\%$,
          confirming that the backdoor requires training-time poisoning and is not a spurious
          detector artifact.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{$N_{\mathrm{burst}}$ Ablation on MM-Fi}

\cref{tab:burst_ablation} and \cref{fig:burst_ablation} report $\SASR(\Nb)$ for
$\Nb \in \{1,\ldots,5\}$.  The results confirm that $\Nb = 2$ is the MM-Fi optimum:
$\Nb = 1$ achieves a single long burst but with more positional variance; $\Nb = 3$ achieves
the same $\SASR$ but each burst is only 10 frames (borderline debounce); $\Nb \ge 4$ falls
below the debounce threshold ($\Wburst < 10$) and is excluded by the $\Nb^*$ criterion
(equation~12).  For other datasets with different $L$ or capture rates, this ablation must be
re-run to find $\Nb^*$.

\cref{fig:defense_audit} audits the susceptibility of our sub-band backdoor to standard
defenses:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Fine-Pruning Mitigation}: Pruning dormant channels in \texttt{out1} suppresses
          attack success, but clean tracking MPJPE degrades sharply beyond $30\%$ channel
          removal.
    \item \textbf{Spectral Signatures Detection}: Because our latent loss trains an isolated
          subspace, the top eigenvalue of poisoned feature representations exhibits detectable
          separation ($\text{AUROC} = 0.801$). This reflects an inevitable security trade-off:
          strong representation steerability leaves statistical traces in the feature covariance.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================================

This paper resolves three empirical and methodological gaps in wireless human pose estimation
backdoors. First, we transition from fragile physical-analog assumptions to a principled digital
supply-chain threat model, formally specifying attacker and defender capabilities
(\cref{tab:threat_model}). Second, our Concentrated Sub-band Trigger ($f \in [20,52)$, $W=32$)
overcomes convolutional downsampling and temporal average pooling in modern backbones (HP-ELI),
providing high attack fidelity without sacrificing digital stealth. Third, the Sliding-Window
Multi-Burst ASR ($\SASR$) framework eliminates positional heurism from evaluation: by averaging
the debounced event ASR over all valid burst-comb placements with step $\Delta$, it produces a
position-agnostic, trigger-agnostic, and dataset-agnostic headline metric. The accompanying
$\Nb$-Burst Ablation (\cref{tab:burst_ablation}, \cref{fig:burst_ablation}) quantifies how many
bursts are needed for a given dataset and capture rate, enabling practitioners to transfer the
evaluation protocol to MM-Fi, UT-HAR, CSIFALL, WiAR, and beyond without re-engineering the
metric.
